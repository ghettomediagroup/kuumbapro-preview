Nine principles for navigating complex social systems

The JEBBEDIAH Way

“These principles are not personality traits to develop or values to perform. They are orientations—ways of relating to yourself, to others, and to the systems you move through.”

Each principle builds on the regulation established through the Joy Up 30 Second Joy Reset™. The reset restores access. The principles help you decide what to do with that access.

The principles are offered in sequence, not hierarchy. They work together, reinforcing one another. Joy opens the door. Healing helps you remain whole once inside.

Every principle below carries the reflection questions and the practice exactly as Dr. Hinton wrote them. Open each one to read them.

J

Joy

Joy opens access.

Why we lead with joy. The JEBBEDIAH Way begins with joy because joy restores access. Joy is not a reward for surviving pressure or performing well. It is a birthright, one of the earliest signals that we belong, that we are connected, that we matter.

Practiced intentionally, joy creates just enough safety for breath, awareness, and agency to return. Not comfort. Not ease. Access. Leading with joy is not avoidance. It is reclamation. It says: I do not have to abandon myself to participate.

Reflection and practice

Reflection questions

  • Where has joy been quietly discouraged in my environment?
  • What changes when I allow joy to be present, even briefly?

Practice

Try the Joy Up 30 Second Joy Reset™ before your next interaction that carries weight.

E

Education

Education provides context.

What this means. Education, in the JEBBEDIAH Way, is not limited to school, credentials, or formal training. The word education comes from the Latin educere, meaning to draw out, to bring forth what already exists within.

Education means learning how power, history, roles, and incentives operate so you can understand what is happening around you without turning it inward as personal failure. It replaces shame with context and confusion with clarity. It is not about knowing everything. It is about staying curious enough to keep learning.

Reflection and practice

Reflection questions

  • Where am I, and what is happening around me?
  • What keeps showing up, even when the people change?
  • What do I know now about myself that I didn’t know before?

Practice

Choose one recurring challenge you are facing. Instead of analyzing yourself or others, pause and ask:

  • What larger context might this situation be part of?
  • What expectations, incentives, or histories may be shaping responses—including mine?
  • What does this help me make sense of?

Write down one insight that increases understanding and reduces self-blame. Notice what becomes available when context replaces judgment.

B

Blackness

Blackness restores wisdom.

What this means. Blackness exists before dehumanization. Long before systems of enslavement, colonization, and racial hierarchy, Blackness held language, cosmology, rhythm, care, governance, creativity, and relational ways of being. Dehumanization did not define Blackness, it attempted to sever humanity from it.

Because anti-Blackness is global and learnable, it can be practiced by anyone. Its impact extends far beyond Black communities, shaping how creativity is constrained, truth is punished, and humanity is measured. Engaging Blackness as wisdom is not about identity restriction. It is about recovering what systems have trained people, of all backgrounds, to forget.

Blackness reminds us that humanity does not begin with permission.

Reflection and practice

Reflection questions

  • What cultural knowledge has helped me navigate difficulty?
  • Where do I see truth being softened or avoided?

Practice

Name one practice or value rooted in Black cultural wisdom that you already use—whether or not you have ever labeled it that way.

B

Belonging

Belonging creates safety.

What this means. Belonging does not require disappearance. As social beings, humans are wired for connection. Belonging is not a preference, it is a form of social currency. It shapes who is heard, who is trusted, and who is allowed to influence outcomes.

In the JEBBEDIAH Way, belonging is not about fitting in or proving worth. It is about being seen and adding value without erasing yourself. When people feel they must shrink, perform, or abandon their values to belong, participation becomes costly. Energy is spent managing perception instead of contributing insight.

Reflection and practice

Reflection questions

  • Where do I feel seen and valued for who I am, not just for what I produce?
  • Where do I manage myself to be accepted?
  • What changes when I imagine belonging as contribution rather than conformity?

Practice

Identify one place where you can show up more fully this week. Notice:

  • what you bring that adds value
  • what you no longer need to hide or perform
  • how interaction shifts when you engage from We rather than Us versus Them

Belonging grows through honest, mutual participation.

E

Equity

Equity adjusts conditions.

What this means. Equity requires action, not just intention, and it also requires understanding. It begins with recognizing that people are not starting from the same place, even when they are playing by the same rules. Equity is not about taking something away from one person to give it to another. It is about adjusting conditions so that everyone has a fair chance to participate, contribute, and thrive.

Equity shifts the frame from fairness as sameness to fairness as access. Without attention to equity, individuals may feel overlooked, overburdened, or pitted against one another. This undermines trust and weakens collective efficacy.

Reflection and practice

Reflection questions

  • Where do I notice differences in access or support?
  • How do existing conditions shape participation, regardless of effort?
  • What becomes possible when access improves?

Practice

Bring to mind a situation where people are expected to perform or comply. Ask:

  • What conditions shape who can participate fully?
  • What small adjustment could increase access without diminishing others?

Name one action that shifts conditions toward shared capacity.

D

Discernment

Discernment sharpens responsibility.

What this means. Discernment is the ability to recognize what is yours to influence, what is yours to support, and what is not yours to carry. It is not indifference. It is not avoidance. It is wisdom in action.

Discernment asks not, Can I do something? But, Is this mine to do? In complex systems, unclear boundaries often masquerade as commitment. Discernment protects energy so engagement can be intentional rather than reactive. It sharpens participation instead of reducing it.

Reflection and practice

Reflection questions

  • What am I carrying that may not be mine?
  • What would it feel like to set that down?

Practice

Before responding to a request, pause and ask: Is this actually mine to carry?

I

Integrity

Integrity preserves coherence.

What this means. Integrity is not about perfection or always making the right choice. It is about being able to live with yourself in the choices you make. Your body often senses misalignment before your mind can explain it.

Integrity includes the willingness to acknowledge harm, take responsibility, and seek repair. This is not weakness. It is ethical strength. When integrity erodes, regret accumulates. When integrity is honored, difficult outcomes can be integrated without self-betrayal.

Reflection and practice

Reflection questions

  • Where do I feel a quiet sense of misalignment?
  • What choice allows me to move forward without regret?

Practice

Before making a difficult decision, practice the Joy Up 30 Second Joy Reset™. From a more regulated place, ask:

  • What action reflects my values, given what I know now?
  • What allows me to remain whole?
A

Authenticity

Authenticity reduces friction.

What this means. Authenticity is not oversharing. It is coherence, the ability to show up in alignment with who you are and what you are doing, without unnecessary performance. In the JEBBEDIAH Way, authenticity is about playing your part.

Performance is rewarded quickly. Authenticity is rewarded slowly. But performance drains energy. Authenticity builds trust. Authenticity is not about standing out. It is about showing up accurately.

Reflection and practice

Reflection questions

  • What feels natural rather than forced?
  • Where do I feel aligned with my role, and where do I perform?

Practice

Choose one interaction where you intentionally play your part rather than perform.

H

Healing

Healing sustains the whole.

What this means. Healing is possible, even here. Healing does not mean the absence of conflict. It means repair, learning, and restored trust over time. In the JEBBEDIAH Way, healing is not about returning to the past. It is about integration—carrying experience without being consumed by it. Healing is not only individual. It is relational.

There are moments when healing requires action. There are moments when healing requires rest. Knowing the difference is wisdom.

Healing is not the end of the journey. It is how the journey becomes sustainable.

Reflection and practice

Reflection questions

  • What does wholeness look like for me right now?
  • Where do I need repair, and where do I need rest?

Practice

Choose one practice from this guide to keep as support. Allow healing to move at a pace that honors both effort and ease.

A way through

Joy is the entry point. Healing is the outcome.

Joy opens access. Education provides context. Blackness restores wisdom. Belonging creates safety. Equity adjusts conditions. Discernment sharpens responsibility. Integrity preserves coherence. Authenticity reduces friction. Healing sustains the whole.
