## Week 2: Education

**Subject:** Education: context, not credentials

**Preview:** The E in JEBBEDIAH. This week, we practice context over self-blame.

Hello,

Last week we sat with Joy, the entry point of the JEBBEDIAH Way. This week I want to walk with you through the E: Education.

When most people hear the word education, they think of school. Degrees. Credentials. The Latin root tells a different story. Educere means to draw out, to bring forth what already exists within you.

In the JEBBEDIAH Way, Education is not about knowing more. It is about seeing more clearly. It is learning how power, history, roles, and incentives operate around you, so you can name what is happening without turning it inward as personal failure.

That distinction matters. When you cannot name the shape of a system, you often blame yourself for what the system was designed to do. Education replaces shame with context. It replaces confusion with clarity. And it keeps you curious enough to keep learning, even when the room does not reward curiosity.

This is not about becoming an expert. It is about pausing long enough to ask a different question.

A reflection to sit with this week:

What keeps showing up in my life, even when the people around me change?

That pattern is usually data. Not evidence of your unworthiness. Not proof that something is broken in you. Just information about the systems you are moving through.

A 30-second invitation:

Before your next weighted interaction, return to the Joy Up 30 Second Joy Reset (TM). Then, from that steadier place, name one thing about the context you are stepping into. Not a judgment. Just a fact. Who is in the room. What their role rewards. What history you are walking into.

Notice what becomes available when context replaces judgment.

## Week 3: Blackness

**Subject:** Blackness restores wisdom

**Preview:** Before it was renamed a category, it was already something.

Good morning.

We are three weeks in. Joy opened the door. Education gave you context for what you were walking into. This week, we sit with the B in JEBBEDIAH. Blackness.

**Blackness restores wisdom.**

Before Blackness was renamed a category to be managed, sorted, or feared, it was already something. It held language. Cosmology. Rhythm. Care. Governance. Creativity. Ways of relating across generations. Dehumanization did not define Blackness. It attempted to sever humanity from it.

To engage Blackness as wisdom is to remember that Black people were not made human by inclusion. Black people arrived here already whole. The wisdom was already here. What came later has often been designed to make us forget that we know what we know.

Sit with that today. Not as a slogan. As a working posture. Where in your week have you been waiting on permission that was never anyone else's to grant?

**A reflection to carry with you**

What cultural knowledge, from your people, your neighborhood, your kitchen, your grandmother's mouth, has helped you navigate difficulty in ways no textbook did?

**A practice, less than thirty seconds**

Name one practice or value, rooted in Black cultural wisdom, that you already use. Whether or not you have ever labeled it that way. Say it out loud if you can. Notice that you have been carrying it the whole time.

The Joy Up 30 Second Joy Reset (TM) is a general wellness practice, not medical or mental health care. It is what allows you to remain present enough to hear the wisdom you already carry.

One quiet note. Anti-Blackness is global and learnable, which is another way of saying it can be practiced by anyone, in any body, inside any institution. Later in this journey, we will sit with how its reach shapes people of every background. For this week, stay with the first move. Blackness restores wisdom that was here before the naming.

## Week 4: Belonging

**Subject:** When belonging costs too much

**Preview:** Being seen and adding value, without erasing yourself.

A note from Dr. Hinton.

There is a specific kind of tired that comes from managing how you are perceived. It is different from the tired of hard work. It builds slowly, and by the end of the day, you have less to give the people who actually need you. You cannot always name why.

This week we sit with Belonging, the second B in JEBBEDIAH. In the JEBBEDIAH Way, belonging is not about fitting in or proving worth. It is about being seen and adding value, without erasing yourself.

Humans are wired for connection. That wiring is not a weakness. It is the reason we can build anything together at all. Belonging becomes costly only when the price of admission is your own coherence. When people feel they must shrink, perform, or abandon their values to belong, energy stops flowing toward the work. It gets rerouted into managing perception.

You already know the feeling. The meeting where you rehearse the sentence before you say it. The room where you soften a truth so it lands easier. The relationship where you have quietly started leaving parts of yourself at the door.

One question to sit with this week.

Where do I manage myself to be accepted?

Not to shame the managing. Just to notice it. Awareness is the first move.

A 30-second practice.

Before your next weight-carrying interaction, run the Joy Up 30 Second Joy Reset (TM). As you enter the room, ask yourself one thing. What do I bring here that I no longer need to hide? Let that question guide what you say and what you leave unsaid.

Belonging grows through honest, mutual participation. It does not require your disappearance.

## Week 5: Equity

**Subject:** Fairness is not the same as sameness

**Preview:** The E in JEBBEDIAH. Notice conditions before you judge outcomes.

We are five weeks in. Thank you for staying with this work.

The E in JEBBEDIAH is Equity. Equity adjusts conditions.

In the JEBBEDIAH Way, equity is not about taking something away from one person to give it to another. It begins with a simple observation. People are not starting from the same place, even when they are playing by the same rules.

That is not an accusation. It is context.

Equity shifts the frame from fairness as sameness to fairness as access. When we only measure effort, we miss the conditions shaping who can participate, and who is quietly carrying more just to stand in the same spot. Without attention to equity, people feel overlooked, overburdened, or set against one another. Trust thins. Collective capacity weakens.

This week, notice conditions before you judge outcomes.

A reflection to sit with:

Where do I notice differences in access or support, in a room I move through regularly?

Not to fix it in one motion. Just to see it. Naming a condition is often the first honest step toward changing it.

A 30-second invitation:

Before your next meeting, project handoff, or family conversation, take the Joy Up 30 Second Joy Reset (TM). Settle. Then, as you enter, ask one internal question. What conditions are shaping who can participate fully here?

You are not solving the whole system. You are widening the aperture. From that place, one small adjustment usually becomes visible. That is enough for today.

Equity is a practice of noticing, then acting where you have influence. It is slow work. It is also honest work.

## Week 6: Discernment

**Subject:** Is this actually mine to carry?

**Preview:** Week 6 of the JEBBEDIAH Way. Discernment sharpens responsibility.

Dear friend,

We are in week six together. The principle this week is Discernment, the D in JEBBEDIAH.

Discernment sharpens responsibility. It is the ability to recognize what is yours to influence, what is yours to support, and what is not yours to carry.

I want to be careful here, because this principle is often misread. Discernment is not indifference. It is not avoidance. It is not a quiet strategy for pulling back from people who need you. It is wisdom in action.

In complex systems, unclear boundaries often masquerade as commitment. We inherit tasks that were never ours. We absorb tensions that belong to a whole structure, not to one person. Over time, the weight of carrying what was never ours starts to look like proof that we care. It is not proof. It is fatigue.

Discernment asks a different question. Not, Can I do something? But, Is this mine to do?

That is a small shift. Its effects are not small. It protects energy so your engagement can be intentional rather than reactive. It sharpens participation instead of reducing it.

**A question to sit with this week**

What am I carrying right now that may not be mine? Name one thing. Not to abandon it, but to see it honestly. What would it feel like to set that down?

**A 30 second practice**

Before your next reply, request, or yes, pause. Take the Joy Up 30 Second Joy Reset (TM). From that steadier place, ask yourself, Is this actually mine to carry? Whatever answer arrives, let it be information, not judgment.

## Week 7: Integrity

**Subject:** The quiet sense that something is off

**Preview:** Week 7 of the JEBBEDIAH Way. The principle your body notices first.

Hello,

Integrity is the seventh principle in the JEBBEDIAH Way. Its litany is short: integrity preserves coherence.

I want to be careful with this word. Integrity is not about perfection. It is not about always making the right choice. It is about being able to live with yourself in the choices you make.

Your body often senses misalignment before your mind can explain it. A tightness in the chest during a meeting. A slow ache after a conversation you did not want to have. That signal is not weakness. It is information.

The JEBBEDIAH Way holds that integrity includes the willingness to acknowledge harm, take responsibility, and seek repair. This is not shame. It is ethical strength. When integrity erodes over time, regret accumulates. When integrity is honored, even difficult outcomes can be integrated without self-betrayal.

A reflection to sit with this week:

Where do I feel a quiet sense of misalignment right now?

You do not have to resolve it today. Naming it is the work.

An invitation to practice:

Before your next difficult decision, take thirty seconds with the Joy Up 30 Second Joy Reset (TM). The reset draws on Laughter Yoga, developed by Dr. Madan Kataria and Laughter Yoga International, as a way to restore access to breath and presence. From that more regulated place, ask two questions:

What action reflects my values, given what I know now?

What allows me to remain whole?

Coherence rarely arrives as certainty. It usually arrives as the quiet recognition that a choice fits, or does not.

## Week 8: Authenticity

**Subject:** Week 8: Showing up accurately

**Preview:** Authenticity is not oversharing. It is coherence.

Some weeks I notice how much energy goes into managing how I appear. Not lying, exactly. Adjusting. Softening. Sharpening. Choosing a version of myself that seems most likely to land.

By late in the day, I am tired in a way that rest does not touch.

That is the cost of performance.

## Authenticity reduces friction

In the JEBBEDIAH Way, the A is for Authenticity. It is not the loudest principle in the sequence. It is not asking you to be more expressive, more transparent, or more visible.

Authenticity is coherence. It is showing up in alignment with who you are and what you are doing, without unnecessary performance.

I say it this way in the guide. Performance is rewarded quickly. Authenticity is rewarded slowly. But performance drains. Authenticity builds trust, in yourself first, and then in the room.

The invitation is not about standing out. It is about showing up accurately. Playing your part.

## One question to sit with

Where in your day do you feel aligned with your role, and where do you notice yourself performing?

Both answers are useful information. Neither is a verdict.

## A thirty-second practice

Before your next meeting, conversation, or task that carries weight, take the Joy Up 30 Second Joy Reset (TM). Not to prepare a version of yourself. To return to yourself.

Then, inside that interaction, choose one place to play your part rather than perform. It may be a smaller move than you expect. Answering a question honestly instead of impressively. Letting a silence stand. Saying what you actually think, in the shape it actually takes.

Notice what changes in your body afterward. Notice what does not have to be maintained later.

## Week 9: Healing

**Subject:** Week 9: Healing sustains the whole

**Preview:** The final principle. How the journey becomes sustainable.

Hello,

We have reached the ninth principle. The H in JEBBEDIAH. Healing.

Over the past eight weeks, we have moved together through Joy, Education, Blackness, Belonging, Equity, Discernment, Integrity, and Authenticity. Each one opened something. Each one asked something. Now we arrive at the principle that holds all of them.

Healing is possible, even here.

Healing does not mean the absence of conflict. It means repair, learning, and restored trust over time. In the JEBBEDIAH Way, healing is not about returning to the past. It is about integration. Carrying experience without being consumed by it.

Healing is also relational. It happens in the small repairs between us, in the moments we choose presence over performance, in the pace we set when the work is long. There are seasons when healing requires action. There are seasons when healing requires rest. Knowing the difference is wisdom.

Healing is not the end of the journey. It is how the journey becomes sustainable.

**A reflection question for this week**

Where do I need repair, and where do I need rest?

**A 30-second invitation**

Return to the Joy Up 30 Second Joy Reset (TM) today. Not to fix anything. Not to prepare for the next hard thing. Simply to remember that access is available, and that you are allowed to move at a pace that honors both effort and ease.
